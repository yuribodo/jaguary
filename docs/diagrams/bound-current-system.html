01 · Architecture · Secure paved road

Executable MVP architecture

The privileged path runs through the Bound backend. The browser talks only to the API; SerpApi supplies structured Google Flights results, while deterministic services select an offer and maintain checkout, authority, reservation, and payment.

02 · Architecture · Product surfaces

What exists in the frontend

Routes are split among acquisition, real demo operation, and account pages. The chat workspace consumes backend conversations and leads from the selected recommendation to the official flight page; cards and history are explicitly visual demonstrations.

03 · Sequence · Durable chat turn

How a TravelBot turn is processed

The turn is claimed in the database before the model runs. If the same key reappears, the persisted conversation is returned; if the runtime fails, the run is marked without inventing a response.

04 · State machine · TravelBot

Conversation state machine

Tools are released by state. Once intent is complete, the backend ranks compatible options, retains only the best, and prepares approval. Changing a required field afterward clears checkout, mandate, and pending approval.

05 · Sequence · Purchase split in two phases

From offer to receipt without hiding consent

The purchase is split into two sequences for clarity: first the system prepares an approval artifact; then explicit confirmation activates authority and releases Verify and Payment.

06 · State machines · Authority and payment

States that prevent reuse and duplicate charges

Mandates and authorizations have different lifecycles. A mandate expresses delegated authority; an authorization reserves one specific economic action and is consumed by the payment result.

07 · Architecture · Tamper-evident evidence

How the ledger makes the trail verifiable

Every event is sanitized before persistence. The payload hash and canonical-envelope hash form a chain per subject; receipts are read only after their chain is recalculated and validated.

08 · Architecture · PostgreSQL ownership map

Who persists what

The schema has 16 tables. For readability, they are grouped by responsibility and primary dependencies without pretending the database is one aggregate.

The throughline is simple: natural language may be probabilistic; authority, consent, reservation, payment, and evidence may not.
