# Yuno — Crypto, Stablecoin & Web3 Strategy

**Research Date:** August 29, 2026  
**Scope:** Cryptocurrency, stablecoins, USDC, USDT, blockchain, Web3, digital wallets, crypto payments, on/off ramps, cross-border settlement, Stellar, Ethereum, Solana  
**Focus:** Classify relationship with each technology (CORE STRATEGY, EMERGING, EXPERIMENTAL, PARTNERSHIP ONLY, NOT RELEVANT, UNKNOWN), determine if crypto is strategically important or merely adjacent capability

---

## Executive Summary

Yuno's relationship with crypto, stablecoins, and Web3 is **partnership-led and use-case driven** — not core infrastructure strategy. The company has announced four major crypto/stablecoin partnerships (Crypto.com Pay, BVNK, Triple-A, Conduit) between January-May 2026, enabling merchants to accept crypto payments and use stablecoin rails for cross-border settlement. However, these are all **partnership-only integrations** — Yuno does not operate its own blockchain, issue stablecoins, or build native crypto infrastructure. Crypto is **strategically important as adjacent capability** (cross-border settlement, merchant crypto acceptance) but **not core to Yuno's identity** as "AI-native operating system for global payments and financial services." Stablecoins (particularly USDC) are treated as **payment rails** (like Pix, UPI, SEPA Instant) — not as speculative assets or Web3 primitives. Yuno's crypto strategy is pragmatic: enable merchants to accept crypto via partners (Crypto.com, BVNK, Triple-A) without managing volatility or blockchain complexity, and use stablecoin rails (Conduit) for faster, cheaper cross-border settlement. There is **no evidence** of Yuno building on specific blockchains (Stellar, Ethereum, Solana) — partnerships abstract blockchain layer from merchants. Crypto is **EMERGING** strategic capability (via partnerships), not CORE STRATEGY. [196][197][198][204][205][206][207][208][209][210][211][212][213][214][215][216][217][218][219][220][221][222][223]
