## Executive Findings

### Nauta’s 2026 strategy is to become the AI-native operational brain for global trade: unify a customer’s fragmented operational data, deploy purpose-built agents on top, prove financial ROI quickly, and expand from supply-chain execution toward industrial scale and payment services.

**Classification:** FACT + INFERENCE  
**Confidence:** HIGH

Nauta’s August 2026 strategic-investment announcement says the company will use new capital for global expansion, AI-native technical hiring, deeper infrastructure for its operational brain, and new global-trade sectors including payment services.[page:1] CEO Valentina Jordan’s 2026 interview adds the company’s go-to-market and product logic: sell to mid-market shippers with acute pain, start engagements with cost-reduction KPIs, then prove revenue generation through fill-rate optimization and cash-to-cash improvements.[page:2] **INFERENCE:** Nauta’s strategy is therefore not “add AI to logistics,” but build a data-and-agent operating layer that becomes increasingly indispensable to supply-chain, procurement, and finance operations.[page:1][page:2]

## Top 10 Strategic Priorities

### 1. Build the unified global-trade data layer

**Evidence — FACT:** Nauta says its operational brain connects systems, documents, and email into a single AI-native data layer containing SKUs, contracts, lanes, workflows, and company-specific context.[page:1] The investment will fund deeper ability to ingest, structure, and activate unstructured operational knowledge.[page:1]

**Why it matters:** AI quality, reliability, and actionability depend on knowing what an event means in the customer’s specific environment.

**Confidence:** High.

**Hackathon implication — INFERENCE:** Do not build a generic prompt interface. Model operational entities and relationships: PO, SKU, supplier, shipment, invoice, contract/rate card, exception, payment, and approval.

### 2. Make agents perform real operational work

**Evidence — FACT:** Nauta says it deploys 20 purpose-built agents that autonomously execute inventory, logistics, and procurement workflows, while its product pages enumerate agents for supplier reliability, price drift, stockout defense, freight anomalies, landed cost, and shipment monitoring.[page:1][page:3]

**Why it matters:** The company’s category claim depends on doing work, not merely generating insight.

**Confidence:** High.

**Hackathon implication — INFERENCE:** Build an agent loop: detect → gather evidence → assess impact → prepare/perform a bounded action → request approval → record outcome.

### 3. Turn “visibility” into exception-to-action execution

**Evidence — FACT:** Nauta differentiates itself from visibility platforms by claiming agents do post-alert work—supplier chasing, document resolution, inventory protection, and cost/margin defense.[page:5][page:3]

**Why it matters:** Knowing a shipment is late creates little value if the organization cannot make a better decision fast enough.

**Confidence:** High.

**Hackathon implication — INFERENCE:** Demonstrate a real response to an event rather than a prettier dashboard: for example, a freight invoice hold, document-correction request, alternative supplier request, or inventory reallocation recommendation.

### 4. Prove economic buyer ROI, not only user productivity

**Evidence — FACT:** Jordan says Nauta starts each engagement with three or four cost-reduction KPIs—penalties, reconciliation time, manual labor automation—then transitions to revenue generation through fill-rate and cash-on-cash improvements.[page:2] The company reports 37% operational hours reclaimed per operator per week, $3M avoided detention/demurrage, a $300K avoided invoice discrepancy, and $1.5M in prevented stockouts for a customer.[page:1]

**Why it matters:** This changes Nauta from a “nice-to-have” operational tool to a budget-justified growth and margin platform.

**Confidence:** High on stated strategy; medium on vendor-reported outcome generalizability.

**Hackathon implication — INFERENCE:** Put dollar impact and operating KPI impact on every action: savings, cash released, prevented stockout revenue, penalty avoided, fill-rate delta, or time saved.

### 5. Target mid-market shippers with enterprise-grade pain

**Evidence — FACT:** Jordan says Nauta moved away from smaller shippers toward companies selling about $200M to a couple billion in revenue, with 600+ suppliers across 40+ countries on average.[page:2]

**Why it matters:** This segment has severe operational complexity but lacks the transformation staff and tolerance for long enterprise-suite rollouts.

**Confidence:** High.

**Hackathon implication — INFERENCE:** Favor workflows that can be adopted by lean operations/finance teams and do not require a multi-year ERP replacement.

### 6. Expand into industrial and manufacturing supply chains

**Evidence — FACT:** BMW i Ventures, Bosch Ventures, Hitachi Ventures, and Yamaha Motor Ventures led Nauta’s 2026 strategic investment; BMW explicitly cited automotive supplier networks and Hitachi/Bosch emphasized industrial AI and fragmented global supply chains.[page:1]

**Why it matters:** It expands Nauta’s ICP beyond importer/distributor roots toward high-complexity industrial supply networks.

**Confidence:** High.

**Hackathon implication — INFERENCE:** Industrial-grade ideas—supplier continuity, parts availability, contract compliance, delayed inbound components, quality/compliance documents, and working capital—are strategically aligned.

### 7. Expand toward payment services and financial controls

**Evidence — FACT:** Nauta says the 2026 round will support expansion into payment services; Axios reported that Nauta is moving into handling supply-chain payments as a major growth area.[page:1][page:6]

**Why it matters:** The company already owns much of the evidence needed for payment decisioning: POs, invoices, shipment records, receipts, contracts, tariffs, freight charges, and working-capital signals.

**Confidence:** High that it is an emerging strategy; low-medium on exact product roadmap.

**Hackathon implication — INFERENCE:** The most compelling Yuno-connected projects tie operational evidence to payment authorization, payment holds, disputes, scheduling, claims, and cash-cycle decisions.

### 8. Scale repeatable deployment and integrations

**Evidence — FACT:** Nauta says enterprise customers can be live within two weeks of kickoff, with first automated workflows in five to seven days; its Forward Deployed Engineer role focuses on production integrations, agent workflow tuning, output evaluation, and reusable playbooks.[page:1][page:7]

**Why it matters:** Fast deployment is central to its competitive advantage over heavyweight planning and ERP programs.

**Confidence:** High.

**Hackathon implication — INFERENCE:** Make the prototype integration-first: show a thin data contract, mocked but realistic ERP/TMS/WMS/payment inputs, and a clear deployment path.

### 9. Preserve human trust through familiar UX and approval boundaries

**Evidence — FACT:** Jordan says Nauta deliberately built Excel-like tables and workflows because conservative operators needed a “Chevy” they understood rather than a novel “Tesla” interface.[page:2] Nauta also states that agents act on routine work while humans decide consequential commitments involving money, suppliers, and customers.[page:3]

**Why it matters:** In global trade, adoption and governance are as important as intelligence quality.

**Confidence:** High.

**Hackathon implication — INFERENCE:** Use familiar operational views, explicit evidence, approval buttons, confidence levels, and audit trails; avoid unexplained autonomous commitments.

### 10. Focus sharply despite global demand

**Evidence — FACT:** Nauta operates in seven countries and supports suppliers across 60 countries, yet Jordan says the company chose to focus exclusively on the U.S. for 2026 because the U.S. mid-market opportunity alone is large enough and geographic expansion adds execution complexity.[page:1][page:2]

**Why it matters:** The company is choosing depth, repeatability, and product maturity over premature geographic sprawl.

**Confidence:** High, with an apparent nuance between operating footprint and commercial focus.

**Hackathon implication — INFERENCE:** Build for globally relevant trade workflows, but design the go-to-market and first integration around a focused initial ICP rather than “all global trade.”

## Top 5 Strategic Opportunities

### 1. Payment decisioning built from trade-operational evidence

**Evidence — FACT:** Nauta is expanding toward payment services and already has invoice, PO, receipt, document, freight, supplier, and inventory context.[page:1][page:3]

**INFERENCE:** Nauta can become the control layer that decides whether a payment should be approved, held, disputed, scheduled, partially released, or escalated, while a payment provider executes settlement.

**Hackathon opportunity:** Build a TradePay Guard agent linking PO × invoice × B/L × packing list × receipt × rate card × payment status into an auditable approve/hold/dispute workflow.

### 2. Cross-functional exception agents

**Evidence — FACT:** Nauta agents already cover inventory, suppliers, logistics, and commercial protection.[page:3]

**INFERENCE:** The strongest product expansion is across boundaries: a delayed shipment affects stockout risk, which affects customer commitments, which affects buying decisions and payment timing.

**Hackathon opportunity:** Delay-to-stockout-to-cash-impact agent that recommends an inventory, supplier, and payment action together.

### 3. Industrial supplier-network resilience

**Evidence — FACT:** BMW, Bosch, Hitachi, and Yamaha Motor Ventures invested based on automotive and industrial supply-chain complexity.[page:1]

**INFERENCE:** Nauta can win industrial use cases where late components, supplier performance, contracts, inventory, and production commitments need one operational model.

**Hackathon opportunity:** Component continuity agent that identifies a delayed critical part, scores production risk, ranks alternate suppliers, and prepares compliant action/approval packages.

### 4. Agent governance and observability

**Evidence — FACT:** Nauta emphasizes human decisions for consequential actions, while its technical hiring emphasizes output evaluation, context design, live monitoring, and failure-mode improvement.[page:3][page:7]

**INFERENCE:** Governance tooling becomes increasingly valuable as Nauta’s agents expand into financial and supplier commitments.

**Hackathon opportunity:** A policy and audit console: agent authority limits, evidence provenance, confidence, approval routing, expected financial impact, and post-action outcome tracking.

### 5. Rapid onboarding from unstructured operations

**Evidence — FACT:** Nauta’s investment use-of-proceeds includes ingesting and activating unstructured knowledge, including account-manager preferences; it already emphasizes email, PDFs, and documents as data sources.[page:1]

**INFERENCE:** The company needs increasingly efficient ways to discover operational rules from messy sources without months of manual configuration.

**Hackathon opportunity:** An “operational playbook extractor” that turns documents, emails, spreadsheets, and historical exceptions into proposed rules, validation checks, and agent approval policies.

## Top 5 Areas Nauta Probably Does Not Prioritize

### 1. Consumer-facing logistics experiences

**INFERENCE:** Nauta’s buyer and product are B2B operational teams, CFOs, owners, and supply-chain leaders; consumer shipment-tracking UX is unlikely to be a strategic focus.[page:2][page:1]

### 2. Generic chatbot or content-generation features

**FACT:** Jordan explicitly says isolated document extraction, email drafting, simple ETA prediction, and chat interfaces are useful but not transformative without operational context and end-to-end logic.[page:4]

### 3. Replacing ERP/TMS/WMS wholesale

**FACT:** Nauta positions itself as an overlay that connects existing systems rather than a rip-and-replace core-system vendor.[page:1][page:7]

### 4. Small shippers with low-acuity pain

**FACT:** Nauta initially targeted smaller shippers but moved toward the $200M–$2B segment because smaller-company pain was not sufficiently acute to transform the industry or justify its motion.[page:2]

### 5. Unbounded autonomous decision-making

**FACT:** Nauta says agents can act on routine work, but commitments involving money, suppliers, and customers remain under human decision/approval.[page:3]

## Geographic and Market Focus

Nauta’s geographic strategy contains an important nuance. **FACT:** The product operates across 60 countries and Nauta says it supports global shippers, distributors, and supply-chain operators.[page:1] **FACT:** Jordan also says the company made a 2026 decision to focus exclusively on the U.S. commercially, even though it had operations in seven countries and inbound interest elsewhere.[page:2]

**INFERENCE:** This is not a contradiction. Nauta is building globally applicable trade infrastructure while concentrating customer acquisition, product iteration, and operational resources in the U.S. mid-market to sharpen execution. Its LATAM history, customer roots, and the NextWave program remain important ecosystem assets, but they do not necessarily mean equal 2026 GTM focus.

## Competitive Positioning

Nauta’s public strategy differentiates against three adjacent categories:

- **Visibility platforms:** tracking tells users something is late; Nauta wants agents to resolve the downstream work.[page:5]
- **Planning platforms:** planning tools model the plan; Nauta emphasizes live operational action when the plan breaks.[page:5]
- **Generic AI products:** generic models or workflows lack customer-specific operational context, business rules, and measurable execution capability.[page:4][page:1]

**INFERENCE:** The company’s desired strategic position is a system of action sitting above systems of record and alongside planning systems—an AI-native operating layer that continuously reconciles reality, calculates consequence, and advances the work under governance.

## Final Answer

### If Nauta’s CEO gave a hackathon team one sentence saying “Build something that helps us achieve ______,” the blank would most likely be:

> **“a unified operational brain that turns fragmented global-trade data into trusted, measurable, human-governed AI actions across supply chain and payments.”**

**Why this is the strongest answer:**

- **FACT:** Nauta’s explicit mission is to create a unified data layer and AI workforce so companies can participate in global trade with intelligence and control.[page:1]
- **FACT:** Its product strategy is data layer first, agents second, with agents operating on contextualized SKUs, contracts, lanes, documents, emails, workflows, and operational knowledge.[page:1][page:4]
- **FACT:** Its commercial strategy demands measurable cost and revenue outcomes, not just productivity demos.[page:2]
- **FACT:** Its 2026 capital plan includes expansion into payment services.[page:1][page:6]
- **INFERENCE:** The highest-alignment hackathon project must therefore combine operational context, a bounded agentic action, a financial outcome, and explicit governance.

## Recommended Hackathon Brief

**Build:** A policy-governed, payment-aware trade-exception agent.

**Inputs:** PO, invoice, packing list, bill of lading, shipment milestone, supplier/carrier contract, rate card, receipt, inventory exposure, and payment status.

**Agent workflow:**

1. Detect a discrepancy or exception.
2. Reconcile evidence across systems and documents.
3. Quantify exposure: cash, margin, fill-rate, penalty, or delay risk.
4. Recommend or draft a hold, dispute, release, supplier chase, claim, or PO adjustment.
5. Route high-impact action through explicit human authorization.
6. Log the decision, evidence, policy, outcome, and learning signal.

**Why it fits:** It directly advances Nauta’s data-layer-first, execution-oriented, ROI-measured, payment-adjacent strategy while complementing Yuno’s payments infrastructure.[page:1][page:2][page:3][page:6]

## Risks / Weaknesses

1. Nauta’s public claims on agent deployment, hours reclaimed, savings, and onboarding speed are predominantly company or investor-release statements; they establish strategic intent but are not independently audited across the customer base.[page:1][page:3]
2. Payment services are explicitly an emerging direction, but exact product scope, partner model, regulatory posture, and launch timing are not public.[page:1][page:6]
3. Nauta’s strategy spans data infrastructure, agents, supply-chain execution, industrial expansion, and payments—ambition that can create focus, integration, and enterprise-delivery risk. **INFERENCE**
4. The human-approval boundary is strategically wise but may limit the speed of autonomous ROI until Nauta proves trust and policy control at scale. **INFERENCE**
