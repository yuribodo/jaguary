## Executive Findings

### 1. Nauta’s strongest documented customers are Berríos and Windmar; both are import-dependent Caribbean operators with direct economic exposure to shipping disruption, poor inventory availability, manual coordination, and cost leakage.

**Classification:** FACT  
**Confidence:** HIGH

Berríos is a major Caribbean home-goods retailer with more than 23 stores, its own distribution and delivery operation, thousands of imported containers annually, 1,300-plus products, 131 suppliers, 30-country operations, 67 trade lanes, and 26 monitored ocean carriers.[page:1] Windmar Home operates in Puerto Rico and Florida, installs more than 100 solar systems daily, and depends on continuous availability of solar panels and batteries.[page:2] In both cases, Nauta is used as a shared operational layer for fragmented logistics data, supplier coordination, shipment visibility, inventory planning, and exception management.[page:1][page:2]

**Sources:**

- [How Windmar Eliminated Stockouts and Scaled Solar Installations](https://www.getnauta.com/case-studies/windmar)

### 2. Customers appear to pay Nauta for operational execution capacity—not merely for visibility—because the measurable value is reduced manual coordination, protected fill rate, lower penalties, and better commercial decisions.

**Classification:** FACT + INFERENCE  
**Confidence:** HIGH

Berríos reports a 46% increase in container volume within two months, automation of more than 40,000 emails and 20,000 documents, a 107.6% increase in processed data volume, 70% lower penalties, at least 20 hours per week saved in a three-person department, and a five-day reduction in ETA-versus-ATA gap.[page:1] Windmar reports fill rate rising from 74% to 90%, no stockouts since adoption, and at least 50% productivity improvement by replacing coordination across 10–15 fragmented parties with a consolidated platform.[page:2] **INFERENCE:** These outcomes justify enterprise or mid-market SaaS spending because they are tied to concrete margin, service, labor, and growth outcomes rather than a discretionary reporting feature.[page:1][page:2]

## Customer Deep Dives

### Mueblerías Berríos

**Company and context**  
Berríos is a leading Puerto Rican home-furnishings retailer with a multi-store footprint, imports primarily from Asia, and operates its own distribution and delivery capability.[page:1] Its supply chain includes high container volume, 1,300-plus products, 131 suppliers, 30-country operations, 67 trade lanes, and dozens of ocean carriers.[page:1] A second Nauta case study describes 28 stores and the largest distribution center in Central America, placing the company in a high-complexity import/distribution environment.[page:6]

**Previous workflow**  
To identify a shipment’s status and business impact, staff had to make calls, inspect multiple systems, contact external companies, and gather documentation manually.[page:1] Bookings were handled partly by hand and partly through an external coordinator, while freight savings were undermined because free-time clocks and carrier performance were not managed in one operational view.[page:6]

**New workflow with Nauta**  
Nauta consolidates fragmented communications, documents, internal data, and shipment data into a single record; tracks shipments and ETAs; provides carrier and supplier intelligence; flags free-time risk; and automates sailing-date, free-time, and land-pickup handoffs.[page:1][page:6] Berríos can identify provider usage, high-volume origins, carriers driving demurrage, and actual receiving capacity from the integrated record.[page:6]

**Product used**  
The documented product surface includes operational data unification, shipment tracking, document processing, supplier intelligence, ETA and inbound planning, detention/demurrage alerting, cost control, and workflow automation.[page:1][page:6]

**Measurable results and ROI**  
Berríos reports 46% higher container volume after two months, 40,000-plus emails and 20,000 documents automated, 107.6% higher processed-data volume, and 70% lower penalties.[page:1] It also reports at least 20 hours per week saved in a three-person department and a five-day lower gap between ETA and actual arrival.[page:1] In the demurrage-focused study, Berríos says it spends $3 million less per year on demurrage, has 65% less manual work, and operates three active automations for sailing dates, free time, and pickup handoffs.[page:6]

**Actual customer job-to-be-done**  
**INFERENCE:** Berríos pays Nauta to turn container logistics from a manually coordinated, penalty-prone cost center into an operational control system that protects margins and inventory availability as volume grows.[page:1][page:6]

### Windmar Home

**Company and context**  
Windmar Home is one of the Caribbean’s largest renewable-energy companies, operating in Puerto Rico and Florida and installing more than 100 solar systems daily.[page:2] Its ability to install depends on continuous supply of solar panels and batteries, making stockouts an immediate revenue and customer-service threat.[page:2]

**Previous workflow**  
Windmar traditionally had to coordinate with 10–15 separate parties to obtain equipment, creating delays and operational complexity.[page:2] Fragmented supplier, shipment, and inventory information meant that the business had less confidence in supply availability while trying to scale installations.[page:2]

**New workflow with Nauta**  
Nauta serves as a single source of truth, consolidates data, coordinates suppliers, supplies accurate port-to-warehouse tracking, provides predictive alerts and trend analysis, and gives teams supplier-performance, inventory, and operational-efficiency intelligence.[page:2]

**Product used**  
The documented product surface is inventory intelligence, stockout defense, shipment visibility, supplier coordination, predictive disruption alerts, and business intelligence for supply-chain decisions.[page:2]

**Measurable results and ROI**  
Windmar says fill rate increased from 74% to 90%, it has not run out of stock since implementation, and productivity increased by at least 50%.[page:2] It also says the same workload can now be handled with fewer resources, which is a direct operating-leverage outcome.[page:2]

**Actual customer job-to-be-done**  
**INFERENCE:** Windmar pays Nauta to keep installation-critical inventory available while scaling throughput without proportionally increasing coordination headcount.[page:2]

### Customers with strongest need

**INFERENCE:** Nauta’s strongest-fit customers have six characteristics:

1. They **own the cargo**, inventory risk, and customer-service outcome rather than merely brokering freight.
2. They import containers or coordinate multi-country inbound supply at meaningful scale.
3. They run many SKUs, suppliers, carriers, documents, and handoffs.
4. Stockouts, port delays, invoice errors, or penalties directly affect margin and revenue.
5. Their existing ERP/TMS/WMS environment is functional but fragmented across teams and external data sources.
6. They need a rapid overlay rather than a multi-year systems replacement.[page:1][page:2][page:3][page:6]

This fits Berríos and Windmar closely, while Sinbiotik suggests expansion into regulated, production-sensitive raw-material supply chains.[page:1][page:2][page:5]

## Strategic Customer Analysis

### 1. What do customers actually pay Nauta for?

**FACT:** Customers use Nauta for unified operational data, shipment tracking, document automation, supplier intelligence, inventory reliability, cost/penalty control, and proactive exception handling.[page:1][page:2][page:6]

**INFERENCE:** Customers actually pay for a reduction in the coordination burden of global trade: fewer people chasing status, less time reconciling documents, earlier action on inventory and shipment risk, and fewer avoidable losses at the boundary between logistics and finance.[page:1][page:2][page:6]

### 2. Which pain points justify enterprise spending?

The pain points severe enough to justify material B2B spend are:

- **Stockouts and lost availability:** Windmar’s fill-rate gain and elimination of stockouts tie directly to installation capacity and customer delivery.[page:2]
- **Detention and demurrage:** Berríos reports $3M less annual demurrage spend, demonstrating a direct and measurable margin leak.[page:6]
- **High manual coordination load:** Berríos removed 20-plus hours per week in one small department and automated tens of thousands of emails/documents.[page:1]
- **Shipment delay and forecast unreliability:** Berríos cut the ETA-to-actual-arrival gap by five days, improving planning across downstream functions.[page:1]
- **Supplier and cost-performance opacity:** Berríos uses supplier intelligence across 131 suppliers in 30 countries, which supports sourcing and margin decisions.[page:1]

### 3. Which problems are operational rather than informational?

**Operational problems** are those where knowing the status is not enough: containers need pickup/return before free time ends; a shipment delay needs inventory mitigation; a document mismatch needs correction; an invoice needs hold/dispute; a supplier failure needs backup activation; a PO needs creation or adjustment.[page:6][page:1][page:2]

**INFERENCE:** Nauta’s strongest wedge is the movement from “information” to “operational consequence”: connect a signal to a person, deadline, financial impact, and prepared next action.[page:6][page:3]

### 4. Which problems could be solved by AI agents?

| Problem | AI-agent suitability | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Document extraction, matching, and exception routing | High | Inputs are document/data rich, rules can be explicit, and human review can focus on outliers.[page:1] |
| Shipment-delay and ETA risk triage | High | Agents can join carrier signals to POs, inventory, lead times, and customer commitments.[page:1][page:6] |
| Demurrage/free-time prevention | High | Deadlines and container events are structured; actions can be alerted, prioritized, and handed off.[page:6] |
| Invoice/freight anomaly review | High | Charges can be matched against terms, shipment evidence, and historical baselines.[page:6] |
| Stockout detection and reorder preparation | High | Agents can continuously combine inventory, demand, inbound status, and supplier lead time.[page:2] |
| Supplier-risk scoring and backup preparation | Medium-high | Data may be incomplete and commercial choices are strategic, but monitoring and pre-work are automatable.[page:1] |
| Supplier replacement and payment commitment | Medium | Agent can recommend and prepare; people should approve contractual or financial commitments. **INFERENCE** |

### 5. Which customer problems intersect with payments?

The clearest intersections are:

1. **Invoice × PO × shipment reconciliation:** A mismatch should block or delay payment until evidence is resolved.[page:1][page:6]
2. **Freight invoice and demurrage recovery:** Carrier charges and penalties can be audited, disputed, credited, or paid based on operational evidence.[page:6]
3. **Landed cost and supplier pricing:** Payment approvals should reflect agreed terms, tariff exposure, quantity received, and actual shipping cost.[page:1]
4. **Working-capital scheduling:** Shipment timing, inventory availability, supplier terms, and payment due dates jointly drive cash conversion. **INFERENCE**, supported by Nauta’s focus on inventory and cost control.[page:2][page:3]
5. **Supplier reliability and payment risk:** Payment status could be linked to contract compliance, OTIF, quality documents, and dispute history. **INFERENCE**.[page:1][page:5]

## Customer Problems That Remain Unsolved

**FACT:** The public case studies show Nauta improves visibility, automation, planning, and cost control, but they do not demonstrate Nauta operating payments, financing, FX, customs brokerage, carrier capacity booking, or direct supplier fulfillment.[page:1][page:2][page:6]

**INFERENCE:** Remaining customer gaps likely include:

- Payment execution and payment-risk controls connected to logistics evidence.
- Trade-finance and working-capital financing decisions.
- Cross-border FX exposure and payment timing.
- Full customs/compliance adjudication across country-specific regulations.
- Counterparty cooperation when suppliers, carriers, terminals, brokers, or warehouses are outside Nauta users’ direct control.
- Broader sales-order, demand, and customer-commitment data required to make optimal trade-offs under disruption.

These are high-value areas because Nauta already controls much of the operational context needed to make safer financial or commercial decisions.[page:1][page:6][page:3]

## Opportunities

### High-value opportunities for Nauta × Yuno

1. **Pre-payment freight and supplier invoice guard:** reconcile PO, invoice, packing list, B/L, receipt, rate card, and payment status; generate a hold/dispute/approve recommendation with evidence.
2. **Arrival-to-pay workflow:** delay or release supplier payment based on customs/document state, receipt evidence, agreed terms, and exception status.
3. **Demurrage claim and recovery agent:** use container, terminal, carrier, appointment, and document records to assemble a recovery packet and payment adjustment request.
4. **Working-capital procurement copilot:** turn inventory, ETA, MOQ, landed cost, supplier terms, and cash timing into a PO-plus-payment recommendation.

**Classification:** INFERENCE. These opportunities align with Nauta’s documented operational inputs and Yuno’s payment-infrastructure positioning, but no public joint product has been announced.[page:1][page:2][page:6]

## Risks / Weaknesses

1. Berríos and Windmar customer results are published by Nauta, so they should be treated as vendor case-study claims rather than independently audited outcomes.[page:1][page:2][page:6]
2. The disclosed customer base is still narrow, with detailed quantitative evidence concentrated in two named case studies.[page:1][page:2]
3. Some customer problems require action by external parties—carriers, ports, customs brokers, suppliers, and warehouses—so software can improve coordination but cannot fully control execution. **INFERENCE**
4. Public evidence of payment execution, trade finance, or regulated financial services remains absent; financial-infrastructure opportunities remain emerging rather than current customer proof.
