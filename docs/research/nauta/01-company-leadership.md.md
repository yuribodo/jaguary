## Executive Findings

### 1. Nauta was founded in 2024 to fix fragmented, manual importer operations in global supply chains.

**Classification:** FACT  
**Confidence:** HIGH

Multiple sources describe Nauta as founded in 2024 by Valentina Jordan and Rafael Santiago, and explain that the initial motivation was the manual, spreadsheet-and-email-heavy nature of import operations, especially in cross-border logistics.[web:8][web:10][web:15] Jordan says she saw logistics as "the last frontier" still lacking digitization, while Santiago ties the founding directly to years of frustration with manual operational work.[web:10] The original problem was not generic visibility; it was reducing operational chaos for importers that lacked a unified, actionable control layer across documents, shipment tracking, ERP data, and communications.[web:19][page:1]

## Detailed Research

### Company

Nauta appears to have been founded in 2024.[web:8][web:15] LinkedIn shows Valentina Jordan beginning at Nauta in May 2024 and Rafael Santiago in March 2024, while 2025 funding coverage describes the company as founded earlier that year.[web:3][web:8][web:15] The most defensible reading is that the company was formed in early 2024 and entered market launch in Q1 2025.[web:15][page:1]

The reason for founding was a combination of firsthand logistics pain and product-building insight.[web:5][web:10] Santiago had spent roughly 14 years in logistics and was frustrated by manual processes and wasted time, while Jordan had built product systems at Rappi and saw logistics as under-digitized despite its economic importance.[web:10][web:15] Nauta’s original problem statement centered on importers managing high-complexity, cross-border operations through emails, spreadsheets, disconnected systems, and scattered documentation.[page:1][web:26]

Nauta’s positioning has clearly evolved. In 2024–2025, independent databases and fundraising coverage framed it as an AI-powered logistics orchestration platform for importers.[web:7][web:13][web:15] In 2026, leadership language shifted toward an AI-native operating system, a data infrastructure layer, and eventually an operational brain for autonomous AI agents in global trade.[web:5][web:6][web:2]

Today, Nauta sells B2B SaaS for supply-chain execution, data unification, and AI-driven workflow automation.[web:3][page:1] Its platform consolidates documents, tracking data, ERP inputs, and email threads; applies business logic at the container level; and surfaces or automates next actions.[page:1][web:6] It has also launched an Inventory Optimization Engine and discusses multiple agents operating on top of a structured data layer.[web:17][web:6]

Its target customers are importers and distributors, particularly mid-market operators with significant complexity but limited enterprise resources.[page:1][web:26] One public description defines this segment as companies with roughly $200 million to $2 billion in revenue, managing hundreds of suppliers across dozens of countries.[web:26] Public customer references and examples suggest industries such as food and beverage, retail, pharmaceuticals, furniture, beauty, and consumer goods.[web:9][page:1]

Geographically, Puerto Rico appears important in the founding story, Latin America is important through the founders’ backgrounds and current brand activity, and the broader commercial opportunity is global trade.[web:10][web:5][web:30] Nauta says it works with importers and distributors across seven countries and positions itself for global expansion.[page:1] Its 2026 hackathon activity across Bogota, Mexico City, Buenos Aires, and Sao Paulo also shows intentional LATAM ecosystem building, even though the company’s commercial narrative is now global.[web:30]

Nauta claims its differentiation is moving beyond visibility into orchestration and execution.[page:1] It says it reconciles fragmented operational data into one control layer, applies shipper-specific business logic, and enables agents to recommend or execute next steps rather than merely report delays.[page:1][web:6] It also claims measurable outcomes, including up to 80% lower detention costs, 30% higher operator productivity, and as much as 75% faster container processing times since launching in Q1 2025.[page:1]

Independent evidence suggests the strongest differentiators are threefold: operator-founder credibility, focus on importer-owned proprietary data, and a narrow starting wedge in complex cross-border workflows.[web:4][web:5][web:15] The FreightWaves interview explicitly argues that AI agents will commoditize and that the true moat is the data foundation underneath them.[web:4] That framing, combined with industrial strategic investors in 2026, implies outsiders see value not just in user experience but in Nauta’s potential role as foundational workflow infrastructure for trade operations.[web:2]

#### Valentina Jordan

Jordan is the public face of Nauta and the most visible strategic storyteller.[web:5][web:6][web:25] Her prior major company is Rappi, where Nauta and third-party coverage say she led product or product-and-engineering teams for a major business segment and helped scale core systems in one of Latin America’s first unicorns.[web:5][web:13] Her technical background is product and technology rather than classical supply-chain operations, and she explicitly says she came into global supply chain through Nauta.[web:5][web:6]

Her entrepreneurial experience before Nauta is not clearly documented in the available sources, so prior startup founding should be treated as unknown rather than assumed.[web:8][web:6] Major achievements that are publicly visible include cofounding Nauta, raising a $7 million seed round in 2025, winning strategic backing from BMW i Ventures, Bosch Ventures, Hitachi Ventures, and Yamaha Motor Ventures in 2026, and establishing Nauta’s market narrative around AI-native orchestration.[web:13][web:2] Public interviews and podcasts include Let’s Talk Supply Chain, BUILDERS, Silk Road Nexus, Logistics of Logistics-related clips on LinkedIn, and Nauta’s own blog Q&A content.[web:5][web:6][web:25][web:26][web:29]

What Jordan repeatedly talks about is unusually consistent. She returns to the ideas that supply chains move not only goods but also data and money, that importer data is fragmented and underutilized, that AI must be grounded in proprietary operational context, and that execution matters more than visibility alone.[web:4][web:5][web:14][page:1] She also talks frequently about being AI-native, refusing shallow proof-of-concept work, and focusing on customers complex enough to feel pain but underserved by traditional enterprise software.[web:5][web:26]

Her LinkedIn activity, at least as reflected in public snippets, amplifies podcasts and messaging around business clarity, fragmented data, and turning supply-chain silos into revenue and action.[web:25][web:29] That suggests her external positioning goal is not just logistics optimization, but a larger category around operational intelligence for trade.[web:25][web:29]

#### Rafael Santiago

Santiago is the operator-founder whose background provides Nauta’s supply-chain credibility.[web:3][web:4] Public sources describe him as having led a Caribbean importing logistics company and as coming from a multi-generational distribution business in Puerto Rico.[web:4][web:15] This gives him direct supply-chain and importer-side operational experience, especially in food distribution and complex import coordination.[web:4][web:10]

His entrepreneurial experience is strongest through cofounding Nauta, though his experience leading within a family business also reflects owner-operator exposure.[web:3][web:10] Evidence of a formal technical background is limited; available sources point more to operational, purchasing, and logistics leadership than software engineering.[web:3][web:10] That distinction matters because Nauta’s founding DNA combines Jordan’s product/technology skill set with Santiago’s supply-chain operations background.[web:5][web:10]

Public statements from Santiago consistently emphasize wasted time, manual work, and the need to automate the importation process from document handling to systems integration.[web:10] In the Yard Management Solutions partnership announcement, he frames Nauta as predicting upstream issues while partners manage what happens once trucks arrive, showing a practical focus on where Nauta sits in the control stack.[web:22] His repeated themes are logistics operations, automation, system integration, execution, and removing friction from operator workflows.[web:3][web:10][web:22]

### Leadership

Publicly visible leadership beyond the founders is thin in the retrieved sources, which suggests Nauta still presents itself externally as a founder-led company.[web:21][web:23] The accessible media kit and brand-guidelines pages prominently identify only Valentina Jordan and Rafael Santiago in the founders-and-team section.[web:21][web:23] That does not prove there are no additional executives, but it does mean the public narrative is tightly centered on the cofounders.[web:21][web:23]

One important non-executive influence is the investor and partner set around the company. The 2025 seed round included Construct Capital and Predictive, with angels such as Simón Borrero of Rappi, Waikit Lau of RemoteHQ, and Juan Jose Gonzales of Windmar Energy.[web:19][web:16] The 2026 strategic investment from BMW i Ventures, Bosch Ventures, Hitachi Ventures, and Yamaha Motor Ventures is strategically notable because those investors bring industrial and manufacturing adjacency, reinforcing the company’s move toward broader supply-chain infrastructure.[web:2]

### Critical analysis

#### 1. What does Nauta actually want to become?

**FACT:** Nauta says it is the operational brain powering autonomous AI agents for global trade.[web:2] **INFERENCE:** This implies the company wants to become the system of record plus system of action that sits above fragmented enterprise software and below user-facing operational teams.[web:6][web:26] **SPECULATION:** If execution succeeds, Nauta may attempt to become the default AI orchestration layer for importer-centric trade operations and then expand across adjacent stakeholders such as distributors, manufacturers, warehouses, and financial partners.

#### 2. What problems appear to motivate its leadership?

**FACT:** Leadership repeatedly describes supply chains as fragmented, manual, reactive, and overloaded by disconnected systems, documents, and communications.[web:4][web:10][page:1] **INFERENCE:** The founders are motivated less by generic logistics visibility and more by operational waste, margin leakage, and the inability of mid-market importers to act on their own data.[web:10][web:26] **SPECULATION:** Jordan may also be motivated by creating a generational software company in a category she sees as under-digitized but economically central.[web:5][web:25]

#### 3. What does leadership appear obsessed with?

**FACT:** Jordan repeatedly emphasizes data structure, AI-native design, and connecting goods, data, and money.[web:4][web:5][web:14] **FACT:** Santiago repeatedly emphasizes manual workflow elimination and practical automation.[web:10][web:22] **INFERENCE:** Leadership appears obsessed with turning fragmented operational exhaust into machine-actionable infrastructure that can drive faster, more autonomous execution.[web:4][web:6][web:26]

#### 4. What strategic themes repeatedly appear?

The recurring themes are:

- **FACT:** Importer-first wedge and mid-market focus.[page:1][web:26]
- **FACT:** Single source of truth across ERP, documents, emails, and tracking systems.[page:1][web:6]
- **FACT:** Moving from visibility to orchestration and action.[page:1][web:5]
- **FACT:** AI-native architecture rather than retrofitted analytics.[web:5][web:26]
- **INFERENCE:** Building defensibility through proprietary operational data instead of model novelty alone.[web:4][web:2]
- **INFERENCE:** Gradual expansion from shipment execution into planning, ecosystem partnerships, and broader global-trade infrastructure.[web:17][web:22][web:2]

#### 5. What changed in Nauta’s strategy over time?

**FACT:** The visible narrative changed from importer workflow automation to a larger AI-native operating-system story.[web:13][web:5] **FACT:** Product scope also broadened from container-level operational execution into SKU-level inventory intelligence and partner integrations.[web:17][web:22] **INFERENCE:** This suggests the company first used a painful, narrow operational wedge to earn trust and data access, then expanded upward into broader decisioning and autonomous-agent infrastructure.[web:26][web:2]

#### 6. What can be inferred about Nauta’s future direction?

**INFERENCE:** Nauta is likely to continue expanding from importer operations into a more comprehensive operating layer that connects logistics execution, inventory planning, and possibly financial workflows around trade.[web:4][web:17][web:2] **INFERENCE:** Strategic investors from manufacturing and industrial ecosystems imply future depth in enterprise integrations and vertical use cases beyond importer dashboards.[web:2] **SPECULATION:** The strongest long-term path is a control-tower-plus-agent platform where external partners, internal operators, and AI systems all act through the same structured data substrate.

## Strategic Implications

What this means for hackathon strategy:

1. Nauta’s center of gravity is not consumer payments or generic AI; it is high-friction operational decisioning in cross-border trade.[web:2][web:4]
2. The strongest ideas will likely align with agentic execution, structured operational data, and workflow automation rather than surface-level analytics.[web:6][web:26]
3. Because Nauta is partnering with Yuno and engaging LATAM builders, there may be white space at the intersection of logistics, payments, and embedded operational finance for importers and distributors.[web:30][web:4][web:20]

## Opportunities

Potential opportunities discovered:

1. Build tools that connect payment events or cash-flow intelligence with logistics events, matching Jordan’s repeated goods-data-money framing.[web:4][web:20]
2. Build agent workflows for exception handling, dispute resolution, document validation, or next-best-action recommendations, aligned with Nauta’s orchestration narrative.[page:1][web:2]
3. Build LATAM-friendly integrations for mid-market importers and distributors that still operate across email, spreadsheets, ERPs, and messaging fragmentation.[page:1][web:26][web:30]
